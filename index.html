Before we begin

If you chose “anxiety” today, I want you to know something before we go any further.

You don’t have to pretend to be okay here.

I know how exhausting it is to feel like your mind never rests, to have your heart race for no reason, to overanalyze everything, or to be afraid of something you can’t even explain.

I also know how tiring it is to smile while, deep down, you feel like you’re fighting a battle no one else sees.

But I want you to read this calmly:

You aren’t broken. You aren’t losing your mind. And you aren’t alone.

For the one who feels lost

Before continuing, I want you to know something.

Not always feeling lost means that you don’t know what to do with your life.

Sometimes it means that you have been surviving for so long, pleasing others, trying to be strong or pretending that everything is fine, that one day you stop listening.

And it’s scary.

It’s scary to look inside and not recognize who you are.

It’s scary to feel that everyone seems to have a way… except you.

But I want to tell you something that I wish someone had told me before:

Being lost doesn’t mean you’re failing. Many times it means that you are leaving behind a version of you that could no longer carry everything.

For the one who is exhausted

Exhausted?

I don’t think you’re tired just because you haven’t slept enough.

I think you’re tired from thinking so much.

From waking up with a mind full of thoughts before you’ve even opened your eyes.

From trying to keep anyone from noticing how much you’re struggling on the inside.

From smiling when, in reality, you just want to shut out the world for a while.

If you chose this option today, I want you to know something.

You aren’t weak for feeling exhausted.

You’ve been surviving for far too long.

And surviving… is exhausting, too.

So, for a few minutes, stop demanding answers from yourself.

For the one who feels alone

If you chose this word today…

Perhaps it isn’t because you are physically alone.

Perhaps it is because you have felt this way inside for too long.

Because there are days when you are surrounded by people, yet you feel that no one truly sees the weight you carry in your chest.

You smile.
You make conversation.
You go on with your life.

But I want to pause for a moment.

Because there is something I need you to hear.

The fact that you feel alone today does not mean you are destined to stay that way.

It does not mean you are unworthy of love.

It does not mean there is something broken within you.

It means you are human.

And that your heart has long been longing to feel seen, understood, and embraced.

Thank you for making it this far.

For the one who cannot name it

I don’t know what I feel.

Sometimes the hardest part isn’t what you’re feeling.

It’s not knowing.

You search inside yourself for an answer, but everything feels tangled. You’re not sure if you’re sad, anxious, exhausted, overwhelmed… or simply numb.

You keep asking yourself, “What’s wrong with me?” and the silence inside can feel terrifying.

For the one who feels afraid

Afraid

You aren’t always afraid of what is happening.

Sometimes, you are afraid of what might happen.

Afraid of suffering again.
Of breaking apart again.
Of trusting and ending up disappointed.
Of getting your hopes up and losing everything all over again.

Fear isn’t always trying to tell you to run away.
Often, it is simply revealing the wounds that still need to be embraced.

You aren’t weak for feeling afraid.

You have survived things that changed the way you see the world.
And even though your heart feels insecure right now, that doesn’t mean it will stay that way forever.

You don’t have to be brave all the time.

03 — The Author

Nicol
Montoya

I’ve lived with anxiety for as long as I can remember. For a long time, I thought it was something I had to fight, silence, or overcome. But somewhere along the way, I stopped fighting my anxious heart and began learning from it. Through the honest and difficult lessons it brought me, I learned how to love myself more gently — and how to love others more deeply. This book comes from that journey. From everything I felt, everything I feared, and everything I learned while finding my way back to myself.
